PICTURE COMMANDS
================

Two commands show a picture from a BMP file (what is read, and how the
pictures are drawn: images.txt). Both take the whole screen, like the
editors: the last row is a status bar, and Esc, Q or Ctrl-Q leave and
clear the screen. They open any file by its content (it must start with
"BM"), whatever its name. On the desktop, Enter on a picture does the
same in a new task (desktop.txt).
A third command, paint, draws and edits pictures; it is described last.

view                                    a picture drawn with characters
-------------------------------------------------------------------
  view [-n] <file>
Draws the picture with colored characters, so it looks the same in VGA
text mode and in every graphics mode, and a serial terminal shows it too.
Every character cell shows two picture pixels, one above the other. The
colors are the 16 of the theme, the nearest ones, mixed by a pattern
(dithering) so smooth colors do not turn into bands.
  -n  --no-dither   no dithering: only the nearest of the 16 colors
A picture too big for the screen is shrunk to fit (its shape is kept);
a small one is shown as it is, never enlarged. Example:
  view /pictures/colors.bmp

gview                                     a picture in true color
-------------------------------------------------------------------
  gview <file>
Draws the picture with real pixels in its own colors, in the area above
the status bar. This needs a graphics mode ('display 1024x768', see
commands/screen.txt). In VGA text mode there are no pixels, so gview
shows the same picture as 'view' and says so on its status bar for a few
seconds. If the display mode changes while it is open (on the desktop),
it switches between the two kinds of picture by itself. A picture that
fits is enlarged by the largest whole factor (pixels stay sharp), a big
one is shrunk. A serial terminal shows only the status bar. Example:
  display 1024x768
  gview /pictures/gradient.bmp

Keys (both)
-----------
  Esc  Q  Ctrl-Q    leave, and clear the screen
  N  P              the next / previous picture of the folder: the
                    .bmp files of the picture's folder, in name order
                    (byte order: capitals first), going round at the ends
  Right  Left       the same, while the picture is fitted
  PgDn  PgUp        the same, always
  F                 fit to the screen / actual size (view: one pixel per
                    half character; gview: 100%)
  arrows            move a picture larger than the screen, when it is not
                    fitted
gview only:
  +  =              zoom in: 25 50 100 200 400 800 %
  -  _              zoom out (= and _ are + and - without Shift)
  arrows            move the picture by an eighth of the screen
Moving to a file that is no picture shows its name and why on the status
bar; N and P still go on to the next one.

The status bar shows the name (at most 12 characters; ~ marks a cut name),
the size in pixels, "shrunk to P%" when the character view had to shrink
the picture (P is the shown width over the real one, to the nearest
percent), the place in the folder (3/12), and the keys. On a narrow
screen the bar drops the less useful parts.

Errors. A problem is one line, and the shell comes back at once:
  error: view: x.bmp: not found
  error: view: /docs: is a folder
  error: view: x.txt: not a BMP picture
  error: view: x.bmp: compressed BMP is not supported (save it
         uncompressed)
  error: view: x.bmp: 16-bit BMP is not supported
  error: view: x.bmp: this kind of BMP is not supported
  error: view: x.bmp: the file is cut short
  error: view: x.bmp: 5000x3000 is too large (at most 4096x4096)
  error: view: x.bmp: not enough memory (...)
'gview' says the same with its own name. A picture needs its pixels in
memory (4 bytes each) plus the file: 'mem' shows what is free.

Display and theme. Both viewers draw again when 'display' or 'theme'
changes while they are open, e.g. in a terminal of the desktop after
Alt+Tab: 'theme amber' makes 'view' use the amber palette's nearest
colors.


paint                                      draw and edit a picture
-------------------------------------------------------------------
  paint [-s WxH] [file]
A picture editor that takes the whole screen. It works with the keyboard,
and also with the mouse (mouse.txt): everything the mouse does has a key.
  paint                a new white picture, 64x40 pixels, not yet named
  paint pic.bmp        opens pic.bmp if it exists; if not, starts a new
                       white picture that is saved under that name
  paint -s 320x200     a new picture of that size, 1x1 up to 4096x4096
  -s  --size WxH       the size of a new picture (default 64x40). With a
                       file that already exists it is an error.
Names are relative to the current directory. Any BMP that 'view' can open
opens (images.txt); paint always saves 24-bit. Errors are one line, and
the shell comes back at once:
  error: paint: size must be WxH, like 64x40 (up to 4096x4096)
  error: paint: 5000x2000 is too large (at most 4096x4096)
  error: paint: x.bmp exists: -s only sets the size of a new picture
  error: paint: x.txt: not a BMP picture
(and the other messages of 'view' for a file that cannot be opened).
On the desktop it is a task of its own, started by the Paint icon, by P
on the home screen (on the chosen picture), or from a terminal.
A picture takes 4 bytes per pixel while it is open (4096x4096: 64 MB),
and opening or saving needs the file's size again in memory for a moment;
a 24-bit file is 3 bytes per pixel plus 54, and saving needs that much
free disk in one piece (images.txt, "Disk"), else it says "Error: cannot
save pic.bmp: not enough free disk space (needs ...)".

The screen. On the right a panel 14 columns wide, in the last row a hint
bar, the rest is the picture. A picture larger than its area scrolls to
follow the cursor, or can be zoomed out to fit (below). The picture's
cursor is drawn in inverted colors and blinks; it is always visible, also
when the picture is zoomed out. Paint needs at least 40 columns and 12
rows; on a smaller screen it says "Screen too small for paint (needs
40x12). Ctrl-Q quits." The panel, from the top (short screens drop the
lines of information first):
  untitled.bmp*   the name (12 characters; * = changed since saved)
  [P][L][R][O]    the tools, as letters; the chosen one shows as >P<
  [B][I][E][H]    (H is the Hand tool, for panning with the mouse)
  Fill off        Fill on / off
  Size 1          the brush size
  Pen up          Pen DOWN, or for line, rectangle and ellipse Corner -
                  / Corner set; empty for bucket and picker
  FG ## 000000    the foreground and background colors, and their RGB
  BG ## FFFFFF
  16 swatches     the colors; F marks the foreground, B the background
  ## custom       the custom color (the 17th)
  x:12  y:30      the cursor's pixel (one space between the numbers
                  when two would not fit, x:4095 y:4095)
  64x40           the size of the picture
  Zoom x4         the zoom: x4 is 4 screen units per pixel, 1/4 is one
                  pixel per 4 units (a reduced view)
  View 0,0        the picture pixel at the top left of the view (V 0,0
                  if the numbers are long)
  Step 1          the arrow keys' step, 1, 8 or 64

Keys (a key means what it does in the editors: Esc backs out, Ctrl-Q quits)
  arrows            move the cursor by the step; it stops at the edge
  Tab               the step: 1, 8 or 64 pixels, then 1 again
  W A S D           pan: the view and the cursor go up, left, down, right
                    by half of what is shown (at least a pixel), so the
                    cursor keeps its place on the screen (small letters
                    or capitals). With the pen down it draws, like arrows.
  Space  Enter      act: what the tool does at the cursor (below)
  Esc               cancel a line, rectangle or ellipse being drawn, or
                    lift the pen. It never quits.
  P L R O B I E H   the tool: pencil, line, rectangle, ellipse, bucket
                    fill, color picker, eraser, hand (small letters work
                    too). Space or Enter with the hand only shows a hint;
                    the hand drags the view with the mouse.
  F                 rectangle and ellipse: outline / filled
  [  ]              the foreground color: previous / next of the 17
  {  }              the background color: previous / next
  X                 swap foreground and background
  c  C              ask for a color RRGGBB (# optional) and put it in
                    the custom slot, as foreground (c) or background (C)
  +  =   -  _       the brush, 1 to 8 pixels (= and _ are + and - without
                    Shift); also the width of lines and outlines
  PgUp  PgDn        zoom in / out one level: 1/64 1/48 1/32 1/24 1/16
                    1/12 1/8 1/6 1/4 1/3 1/2 x1 x2 x3 x4 x6 x8 x12 x16
                    (text mode stops at x8)
  z                 fit: the whole picture, as when it was opened (the
                    largest zoom that shows it all; at most x8, in text
                    mode x1)
  Z                 zoom x1 (one screen unit per pixel)
  Ctrl-Z  Ctrl-Y    undo / redo
  Ctrl-S  Ctrl-O    save; a new picture asks "Save as:" first
  Ctrl-A            save under another name; asks "... exists.
                    Overwrite? (y/n)" if the name is taken
  Ctrl-N            a new white picture: asks the size ("New size WxH:",
                    the current size is suggested; type to replace it)
  Ctrl-L            open another file ("Load file:")
  Ctrl-Q            quit; with unsaved changes the bar warns, and a
                    second Ctrl-Q quits without saving
Ctrl-N and Ctrl-L first ask "Unsaved changes. Discard them ...? (y/n)"
when the picture changed; only y goes on. In a prompt, Backspace edits,
Enter accepts and Esc cancels. A name without .bmp gets it added. After
an action the bar shows a message until the next key, e.g. "Saved
pic.bmp (64x40)" or "Error: cannot save pic.bmp: ...". Alt keys are not
used, so Alt+Tab and Alt+H of the desktop keep working.

The tools (all stop at the picture's edge; the brush is a square of
Size x Size pixels around the cursor)
  pencil   Space puts the pen down and draws a dot; while it is down every
           move draws a line from the old place to the new one (so steps
           of 8 leave no gaps); Space or Esc lifts it. The panel shows
           Pen DOWN. Pen down to pen up is one undo step.
  eraser   the same, in the background color
  line     Space sets the start, the cursor moves with a preview, Space
  rectangle draws it, in the foreground color; Esc cancels. The ellipse
  ellipse  fits in the box between the two corners. With F on, a
           rectangle or ellipse is solid, otherwise an outline.
  bucket   fills the area of exactly the color under the cursor (up to
           the other colors, not diagonally) with the foreground color
  picker   makes the foreground the color under the cursor (a color of
           the palette selects its swatch, any other goes into the
           custom slot); then the tool before it comes back
Every drawing is kept until undone: Ctrl-Z goes back through the last 16
steps (as far as memory allows), Ctrl-Y goes forward again, and a new
drawing clears what could be redone.

Zoom and pan. A reduced view (1/64 to 1/2) shows each block of pixels as
its average colour, so thin lines are fainter but stay visible; the
cursor and the mouse mean the top left pixel of the block under them.
The mouse pans with the Hand tool (a left or right drag) or with the
middle button (any tool), and the wheel zooms, about the pixel under the
pointer: images.txt, "Zoom and pan" and "The mouse in
paint". A mouse without a wheel or middle button uses PgUp, PgDn and the
Hand tool. The keys PgUp PgDn z Z W A S D do everything the mouse does.

Hint bar. At rest it lists the main keys (on a bar 107 columns wide or
more also PgUp/PgDn zoom, z fit, Z x1 and WASD pan); after the mouse was
used it says " Left fg  Right bg  Middle pan  Space act  ^Z undo ..."
instead (with PgUp/PgDn zoom and WASD pan on a very wide bar, and without
"Middle pan" on a bar under 68 columns). While the pen is down, a corner
is set, a drag is in progress or a question is open, it says what the
keys do now.
