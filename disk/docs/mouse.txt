THE MOUSE
=========

A PS/2 mouse works on the desktop and in paint, and nowhere else. The
terminal, the text editor, the hex editor, the shell and the picture
viewers use the keyboard only: a mouse over them does nothing. Everything
the mouse does can also be done with the keyboard (desktop.txt,
images.txt), and with no mouse at all nothing changes.

Where it works
--------------
  home screen and folders   click, double-click, drag (below)
  taskbar                   click an entry, from any task
  paint                     draw with it, click its panel (images.txt)
  everywhere else           nothing; on the desktop only the taskbar
                            reacts, while a terminal, an editor or a
                            viewer is in front
The left, right and middle buttons are used, and in paint the wheel (a
mouse without a wheel or a middle button loses nothing: paint has keys
and a Hand tool for the same).

The pointer
-----------
It is drawn by the display, on top of everything, and is never sent to a
serial terminal. It is hidden until the mouse is first moved or clicked,
and hidden again when a key is pressed, until the mouse moves again. It
only shows on the desktop and in paint; leaving the desktop (or quitting
paint started from the shell) takes it away.
* VGA text mode: the cell under the pointer is highlighted: its text and
  background colors are swapped. If that would change nothing (both are
  the same) the colors of desktop.selected are used. Anything written to
  the cell meanwhile shows, still highlighted. Over paint's picture there
  is no highlight: paint's own cursor follows the mouse there (its panel
  and bars get the highlight).
* Graphics modes: a white arrow with a black outline, 12 x 19 pixels at
  text size 100%, its tip is the point that counts. Its size follows the
  text size: the percentage divided by 100, rounded down, from 1 to 8
  times. The pixels under it are put back when it moves, so it never
  damages a picture.
'display' and 'theme' changes keep the pointer inside the new screen.

The pointer's position is one for the whole screen. In a graphics mode
one mouse count is one pixel, and the pointer stays inside the text area
(a framed mode keeps its border). In text mode a cell counts as 8 by 16
points, so 8 counts are one column and 16 counts one row. There is no
acceleration. The text cell under the pointer decides what a click hits,
in every mode.

On the desktop
--------------
The home screen and every folder grid (the Files grid too):
  left click on an icon     chooses it, like the arrows. The message row
                            says "Double-click opens it, drag moves it"
                            until the next key.
  left double-click         opens it, like Enter: two left presses on
                            the same icon within 400 ms. A third quick
                            click counts as a single click again.
  left button down on an    picks the icon up, like M, once the pointer
  icon, then moving 4       has moved 4 pixels or more from where the
  pixels or more            button went down. The icon follows the
                            pointer cell by cell (over a full cell the
                            icon there shows in the dragged icon's old
                            cell). Letting go puts it down in the cell
                            under the pointer, swapping with an icon
                            there, and saves the places in
                            /system/icons.cfg, as M does. Letting go
                            outside the grid, or pressing Esc while
                            dragging, puts everything back. Dragging
                            never scrolls the grid.
  click on empty grid       nothing; the chosen icon stays chosen
  space, the header or
  the hint rows
  right or middle button    nothing
The whole 16 by 5 cell of an icon is its target: the box, the name and
the space around them.

The taskbar (the last row), from any task in front:
  left click on Home        goes to the home screen at /desktop (also when
                            a folder grid was open, or is in front now)
  left click on a task      brings that task to the front, like Alt+Tab
  ...                       a click on " ..." (tasks that did not fit),
                            on the gap or on the clock does nothing
An entry's target is its text and the space on each side of it. While
the Alt+Tab list is open, taskbar clicks are ignored.

In paint
--------
The mouse draws: the pointer moves paint's cursor, the left button draws
in the foreground color and the right button in the background color, and
the panel on the right can be clicked (tools, Fill, Size, colors).
The middle button drags the picture (pans it) with any tool, and the
wheel zooms in and out about the pixel under the pointer. For a mouse
without those, the Hand tool ([H] in the panel) pans with the left or
right button, and PgUp and PgDn zoom. Everything is listed in images.txt,
under "The mouse in paint". While a question is open in paint, clicks and
the wheel do nothing.

The mouse command
-----------------
  mouse                 PS/2 mouse: found / not found ("found, with a
                        wheel" for a wheel mouse), the pointer's
                        position (in pixels, and as column and row) and
                        the buttons held
  mouse on              turns the mouse on at once and saves that
  mouse off             turns it off at once and saves that
  mouse -t              prints every event until a key is pressed
'mouse' also says "found, turned off" for a mouse that was turned off,
and "off: not looked for" when the machine started with 'mouse off'.
'mouse on' says "Mouse on (saved in /system/mouse.cfg)", or adds "but no
PS/2 mouse was found". 'mouse -t' is for trying a mouse on a new
computer: "move 10 -5" is 10 counts right and 5 up (down is positive),
and "left down" / "left up", "right down", "middle up", ... are the
buttons. A wheel mouse also prints "wheel N": N notches, negative when
the wheel turned away from you (up), positive towards you. Without a
working mouse it says "no working mouse".

The choice is kept in /system/mouse.cfg, one line, 'mouse on' or 'mouse
off' (# lines are comments); you may edit it. With 'mouse off' the system
does not touch the mouse port at all at boot, which helps on a computer
whose keyboard controller misbehaves with a mouse. The boot screen shows
the result in a line like
  Mouse:        PS/2 found (0 ms)
(also "not found" and "off (mouse.cfg)").

Hardware
--------
Only a PS/2 mouse is supported: it is read from the keyboard
controller's second port (hardware.txt). A USB mouse works only if the
BIOS makes it look like a PS/2 mouse ("legacy USB support"), and not
every BIOS does. Many laptop touchpads are PS/2 and should work. There is
no USB mouse driver. A wheel is used if the mouse has one (it is asked
for when the mouse starts, and paint is the only user). With no PS/2
mouse found
(boot looks for one for well under a second) 'mouse' says "not found",
no pointer ever shows and everything else works as it always did.
QEMU gives its virtual PC a PS/2 mouse by default.
